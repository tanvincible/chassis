# ADR-0025: AVX-512

**Date:** 2026-10-20
**Status:** Accepted (#131)

## Context

At 1,536 dimensions on x86, FAISS's HNSW answered more queries a second than Chassis even with
Chassis's vectors on huge pages: 1.03 to 1.17 times as many on the CPUs with AVX-512. FAISS picks
its SIMD level as it starts, and AVX-512 was worth 4% to it on Zen 4, 8 to 9% on the Xeons and 15%
on Zen 5. Chassis had only AVX2 kernels on x86 (ADR-0006). Rust's AVX-512 intrinsics are stable
from 1.89; the crate's minimum was 1.88.

## Decision

### 1. Searches in full precision use AVX-512

The search loop over vectors in full precision (ADR-0013, ADR-0021) has a copy compiled for
AVX-512F, with an AVX-512 kernel inlined, which it runs on CPUs that have AVX-512F, AVX2 and FMA,
for vectors of 1 KB or more (decision 4). Builds search through it too. The greedy descent through
the upper layers, the build's choice of neighbors, exact scans and half precision stay on AVX2: they
weren't measured.

### 2. The same bits as AVX2

The AVX2 kernel keeps four sums of eight lanes a vector. The AVX-512 kernel keeps them as two sums
of sixteen, lane for lane, and hands them back to the AVX2 code for the components left over and
the final adds. Every sum is added in the same order, so a distance is the same bits on either
kernel: a search finds exactly what it did, and agrees with the code that stays on AVX2, as ADR-0021
asks of grouped distances. The lab's first kernel added its sums in another order; recall was the
same, but a result could differ by a rounding.

### 3. Not on Intel's AVX-512 before Sapphire Rapids

Skylake-SP to Cooper Lake, Cannon Lake, Ice Lake, Tiger Lake, Rocket Lake and Xeon Phi stay on
AVX2, by CPUID family and model (Linux's `intel-family.h`). On the one measured, a Xeon 8370C (Ice
Lake), searches at 768 and 1,536 dimensions lost 2 to 8% and builds on every core took 3 to 9%
longer; at 384 dimensions, a size its A/A arm left out, searches gained 9%. It loses at the long
vectors AVX-512 is for, so it keeps what `main` does. Skylake-SP and Cascade Lake slow their clocks
more for 512-bit arithmetic; the rest weren't measured. The flags can't tell the 8370C from the
Xeon 8573C (Emerald Rapids), which gained: the hypervisor doesn't show the 8573C's `avx512_fp16`.

### 4. From 1 KB a vector

AVX-512 runs only where a search groups its distances (ADR-0021): vectors of 1 KB or more, 256
dimensions in full precision. Below that the loop is `main`'s. In this pull request's benchmark at
128 dimensions × 100,000, a Xeon 6973P-C (Granite Rapids) searched 6 to 8% slower with AVX-512, a
Xeon 8573C 2 to 3% faster and a Zen 5 within 1%; in the run below, Zen 4 and the 8573C gained 3 and
5% at 128 dimensions and Zen 5 nothing. The 6973P-C gained 6% at 1,536 dimensions in the lab. A
loss of 7% outweighs gains of 5% at most, so short vectors keep AVX2.

### 5. Rust 1.89

The intrinsics are stable from Rust 1.89, which is now the oldest supported. Keeping 1.88 would
have taken a build script that compiles the kernel only on newer compilers.

### Left out

- **Half precision.** AVX-512F widens sixteen halves at a time too; not measured.
- **The other x86 kernels**: `euclidean_distance`, the build's choice of neighbors and exact scans,
  which call a kernel through a pointer. Not measured.

## Measurements

First a lab (avx512 RUN 1, 2026-10-19, `bench/lab/avx512-plan.md` on `lab/x86`): through Python at
1,536 × 99,000 after `warm`, an AVX-512 kernel made 1.06 (Xeon 6973P-C), 1.09 (Zen 4), 1.11 (Xeon
8573C) and 1.20 (Zen 5) times as many queries a second, which put Chassis at 1.01 to 1.04 times
FAISS on each.

Then this code against `main` (avx512ship RUN 1, 2026-10-20, `bench/lab/avx512ship-plan.md`), 24
jobs on GitHub's x86 runners. Queries a second at the same `ef` from a reader, one query a call,
the median over `ef` 32 to 256, then of a CPU's clean jobs (jobs); after the comma, with `warm`.
Builds: `main`'s time over this code's.

Every search returned `main`'s results, ids and distance bits, at every size and `ef`, in every
job. Zen 3, with no AVX-512, ran the same code in both and was the control. The run came before
decisions 3 and 4: with them, the Xeon 8370C, and every CPU at 128 dimensions, runs what `main`
does.

The pull request's benchmark (`bench.yml`, one runner a case, five rounds in turns) drew three x86
CPUs at 128 dimensions × 100,000, all before decision 4: Xeon 8573C 1.02 to 1.03, Zen 5 0.99 to
1.00, Xeon 6973P-C 0.92 to 0.94. At 1,536 × 20,000 a Zen 5 made 1.22 to 1.23 times `main`'s
queries a second and built in 0.93 of the time.

## Consequences

### Positive

* On Zen 4, Zen 5 and the newer Xeons measured, searches over vectors of 384 dimensions and more
  are 3 to 24% faster, and builds take up to a tenth less time.
* Results are unchanged, bit for bit, on every CPU.

### Negative

* Rust 1.89 or later is needed to build Chassis.
* The search loop is compiled once more per width, for AVX-512.
* CI tests the kernel only on runners that have AVX-512, about half of its x86 ones.
* Under 1 KB a vector, Zen 4 and the Xeon 8573C give up the 2 to 5% AVX-512 gained them at 128
  dimensions. The 6973P-C was measured at 128 and 1,536 dimensions only.
* Decision 3 rests on one Ice Lake job, which also gained at 384 dimensions, and for the other
  models on how they are known to clock. The 8370C turns up among GitHub's x86 runners now and then
  (three of twenty jobs in one earlier run), so more can be measured. An Intel CPU after Sapphire
  Rapids that loses would need its model added.
